# The Matrox G550

The owner's choice (2026-11-23): the G550 before the Radeon 7000 VE, as the smaller step. The card is in a new
second Pentium 4, bench G (bench F is the other, with the G450), with GLOS on it.

## What it is to the code

- **PCI 102B:2527.** Before this branch nothing in RetroGPU knew the ID: no Matrox device was found on a G550.
- **The G400's 3D path.** Linux's old DRM `mga` driver (MIT-licensed, `drivers/gpu/drm/mga/mga_warp.c` as of
  v5.15) installs the same WARP microcode on a G550 as on a G400 (its `FIRMWARE_G400`, `MGA_MAX_G400_PIPES`)
  and sets the WARPs up the same way (`WIADDR2` and the rest). That driver files the G450 (device 0525 as well)
  under its G400 type.
- **A G450's memory.** The G550 is the G450's successor with DDR memory. The HAL treats it as a G450 after an
  engine soft reset: its memory set up again from the BIOS's PInS v5 block (`g450_memory_prepare`), as the
  G450 needed on bench F (`docs/g450-memory.md`). Bench G decides whether the G550 needs it.
- **Clocks and modes:** the BIOS's. `clocks.c` changes clocks only on a G400 whose PInS v4 block is bench-g400's
  (a G450's or G550's PInS v5 is left alone); modes come from the VBE BIOS as on every Matrox card.

## The changes (branch g550)

- `hal/src/pci.c`: 2527 in the known devices, family G400, "MGA-G550", with the G450's memory set-up.
- `hal/src/rg/mga_glos.c`: GLOS's drawing-engine path maps 2527 to the G400 family (it named it a G100).
- `glide/tools/conform/run.py`: card `g550` draws with the G200's expectations and the WARP's, as the G450.
- `tests/hal/g450mem.c`: G450MEM runs on a G550 too.

## What bench G showed (2026-11-23)

Bench G: a PCChips M925G, Pentium 4, VIA chipset (host bridge 1106:3148, VT8235), the G550 on the AGP bus
(01:00.0, sub 102B:0F84, rev 01), 32 MB. GLOS runs there natively (no DOS under it: the native-dos branch), so
these were also the first DOS/4GW programs on GLOS's own DOS: PROBE and ROMDUMP ran as on a hosted bench.

- **G0.** PROBE: "MGA-G550 id=2527 rev=01", family G400, the new BAR layout (frame buffer dc000000, registers
  dfefc000, ILOAD df000000), VBE 3.0 with 32 MB, mode 111h at a 1024-pixel pitch, the engine's fills and depth fill
  read back right. ROMDUMP: "Matrox G550 VGA/VBE BIOS (V1.4) b14", 36,864 bytes, a PInS v5 block of 128 bytes
  at 7CE0h with the G450's fields (DDR flagged; MCTLWTST 0a81462b as bench F's G450, OPTION 404a1520, MEMMISC
  80000a04, MEMRDBK 01001220), kept in the local ROM set only (`g550-benchg.bin`). RGDEV: the `mga` device, 9
  modes, memory to 32 MB, 200 drawing operations as the model, swap, cursor, close.
- **G1: the G550 needs the G450's memory set-up after a soft reset, without its clock stop.**
  - *Without the set-up* (MODETEST `resets=1 noreinit`): 39,248 of 307,200 pixels wrong at once, 115,472 after 8
    seconds and rising, the engine's fresh fills wrong too. As the G450.
  - *With Linux's whole sequence* (g450_memory_init, a boot-time one: the memory clocks stopped and started), new
    contents hold, but **what memory already held comes back shifted** after about one set-up in three. RSTMEM
    (new: a pattern over 16 MB, read again after each reset): either every dword differs or none; where they
    differ, each address reads what was written 16 bytes on, or 32 bytes back (the first few dwords from further
    away), and a rewrite reads back right. So nothing is lost: the memory's pipeline comes up a beat or two out of
    step with what was stored. On bench F's G450 the same sequence kept a screen 20 times of 20.
  - *The steps tried* (`RSTMEM steps=`, pci.c's `mga_g450_reinit_steps`, 12 resets each): all of them, 4 shifts;
    without the clocks' stop (1FB), or without either clock write (1EB), none and no decay; the mode registers
    alone, with or without the refresh off and on (40, 41), or the refresh alone (1): memory unusable.
  - **So for 2527 the HAL leaves the memory clocks running** (`mga_g450_reinit_steps` 1FB; the G450 keeps 1FF).
    392 resets in all with it: no shift, no decay over 8-second waits. Two of 380 changed about 2 KB each (222 and
    244 dwords, every other dword, a few bits), both inside the buffer on screen (offsets 3E000h and 56000h of a
    640x480x16 screen): a read the display had in flight when the reset came, most likely.
- **Why it mattered:** DOS-GL's first WARP load resets the engine at the first triangle, after the context's white
  texel (fog on untextured triangles) and the first textures are in memory. With the whole sequence: DOS-GL
  conformance 31 of 35 (t10 fog's floors black, t12, t14 textures' last rows, t25). With the clocks left running:
  **35 of 35**, the WARPs' rings write-combining by AGP at 2x, GLOS's write-combining on the frame buffer.
- **Glide** (the Matrox back end as it is; its rings cached, DOS/4GW not finding GLOS on this branch): conformance
  23 of 27 against the Voodoo's references. t22 is the gamma test (the display, which a bench can't capture). t07
  (W buffer, 2.7% for a limit of 2%) and t08 (culling, 0.05%) are just over. **t12's first pyramid is one level
  (the base) where each mipmap level should show as a band**; its second is right. The same with either set-up.
  Glide's suite was never compared with the Voodoo's frames on a real G400 or G450: bench F's G450 says next
  whether these three are the G550's or any real card's.
- **The HAL maps 16 MB of the frame buffer** on every Matrox card (`mga_map`'s default), so PROBE's VRAM probe
  says 16 MB here as on a 32 MB G450; the device (`rg/mga.c`) takes the BIOS's 32 MB.
